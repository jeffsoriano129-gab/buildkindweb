Scope

What maintenance can—and can’t—include

What maintenance can include

  • Routine updates to supported WordPress core, themes, and plugins, where applicable.
  • Backup checks or scheduled backups using an agreed tool or hosting feature.
  • Uptime monitoring and basic availability checks.
  • Basic security and functionality checks.
  • Minor content changes within the time allowance of the selected plan.
  • Communication if a visible issue is identified during routine checks.

What isn’t included

Maintenance is not unlimited development or a guarantee that a website will never experience downtime, security incidents, or software conflicts. Unless agreed in writing, plans do not include:

  • Hosting, domain renewals, email hosting, premium plugin licences, or other third-party costs.
  • New pages, redesigns, custom features, major layout changes, or development projects.
  • Emergency recovery, malware cleanup, hacked-site remediation, or repair of pre-existing issues.
  • Extensive content entry, copywriting, SEO campaigns, or third-party service troubleshooting.
  • Guaranteed response or resolution times beyond those expressly stated in the maintenance agreement.

Maintenance plan notes

The fine print, in plain English

  • Maintenance is optional and begins only after the plan and terms are agreed.
  • Minor-edit time is limited to the allowance stated in the plan; unused time does not roll over unless agreed in writing.
  • Support hours, response targets, payment dates, cancellation notice, backup retention, and included platforms are specified in the maintenance agreement.
  • A site that needs substantial repair before maintenance begins may require a separately quoted cleanup or stabilization project.
  • Availability depends on access to the site, hosting, and required third-party tools.
